# Lean Fundamentals for Coaching

## Start at the work

Describe what was directly observed: where it happened, when it happened, how often it happens, and how the current condition differs from the expected condition. Separate facts from assumptions.

## TIMWOODS

- **Transportation:** Unnecessary movement of materials or information.
- **Inventory:** More material or work in process than is needed now.
- **Motion:** Unnecessary movement by people or equipment.
- **Waiting:** Idle time caused by an unavailable input, decision, person, or machine.
- **Overproduction:** Producing earlier or in greater quantity than downstream demand.
- **Overprocessing:** Work that adds effort without adding customer value.
- **Defects:** Rework, scrap, correction, or information errors.
- **Skills:** Underusing people's knowledge, ideas, or capabilities.

A condition may involve more than one category. Treat the classification as a prompt for investigation, not proof of a root cause.

## PDCA

### Plan

Define the current condition, target condition, evidence, likely causes, scope, owner, and measure of success.

### Do

Run a small, reversible experiment. Record what changed, when it changed, and who participated.

### Check

Compare the result with the baseline and target. Look for side effects, variation, and evidence that contradicts the original theory.

### Act

If the experiment works, decide how to standardize and sustain it. If it does not, capture what was learned and plan the next test.

## Coaching rules

- Ask before assuming.
- Prefer a measurable gap over a vague complaint.
- Do not label a person as the root cause.
- Do not claim savings without a defined calculation and verified inputs.
- Do not recommend bypassing safety, quality, or change-control requirements.
