# Fictional Lean Coaching Examples

All names, processes, and measurements below are invented for demonstration.

## Example: Queue between two work steps

**Observation:** A fictional team normally sees two totes waiting between cutting and assembly. During three observations this week, the queue contained eight to ten totes and the oldest tote waited 42 minutes.

**Possible waste:** Inventory and Waiting.

**Questions:** Did demand, batch size, uptime, staffing, sequence, or material availability change? Is the queue consistent across shifts? What is the downstream takt or pull signal?

**Small experiment:** For one scheduled hour, reduce the upstream release batch and record queue size, throughput, defects, and interruptions every 15 minutes.

**Important:** The experiment is only a draft until the process owner and affected team approve it.

## Example: Repeated walking for a shared tool

**Observation:** A fictional operator walked to a shared cabinet six times during one 25-minute cycle to retrieve gauges used at the same workstation.

**Possible waste:** Motion and Waiting.

**Questions:** Are all gauges required for every variant? Are there calibration, security, ergonomic, or point-of-use constraints? Does another workstation need the same tools at the same time?

**Small experiment:** With owner approval, test a clearly labeled point-of-use location for one shift and compare walking distance, search time, tool availability, and any safety or quality concerns.
